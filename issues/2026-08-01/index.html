webrtc-media  webrtc-data  /  72 commits, one new repo

629 bytes of desktop

The commit sequence is the story, so here it is in order. Monday: SRTP-GCM, RTP and G.711, then full-duplex audio — the box beeps, the browser’s microphone comes back. Monday night: the boolean entropy coder of RFC 6386, the first piece of a codec. Then the 4×4 DCT and quantization. Then a keyframe’s uncompressed header, byte-exact against ffmpeg. Then the first decodable frame — a solid grey keyframe, verified by libvpx. Then an alternating pattern confirmed on a real device: Safari decodes our frames.

Tuesday was correctness and then colour. A bitstream parser was built on the bool decoder purely as a debug oracle; the DC path was verified against ffmpeg, which isolated AC coding as the remaining bug; real images encoded once the end-of-block node and the Y2 AC quantizer were exact; then 4:2:0 chroma. And then the number in the headline.

Inter frames built from exactly one mode — a zero motion vector against the last reconstruction. The prediction is the co-located pixels of the previous frame, with no motion search and nothing on the wire, so an unchanged macroblock collapses to a single skip bit. A static desktop costs 629 bytes instead of 76 kilobytes.

webrtc-media — 8c6bbad

The refinements that followed all sharpen the same idea: use what the desktop already knows. RFB damage rectangles feed the encoder, so a static frame encodes in a millisecond. A per-frame size bound, not just an average rate. An adaptive quantizer with an idle refinement pass, and deferred macroblocks drained when the screen goes quiet. And then, on Friday, one global motion vector per frame that a macroblock may choose instead of the zero one — so a scroll costs a mode bit rather than a re-encode of everything it touched, which is what had been producing seconds of mush after every scroll.

And on the other end, a phone on a cellular network

The client side spent the week on the things that only appear in the field. Two-finger scroll-lock, natural touch scrolling, mid-session quality switching, a cursor ring with a real outline so it shows on light backgrounds, and video that does not swallow touches. Enrolled devices renew themselves for 24 hours, credentials expire visibly, and the device file — not process memory — is the source of truth.

The best-diagnosed failure of the week was an ICE pair that never formed on cellular, from a real session where the box was advertising a relay and the phone offered three of its own. Gathering was calling install-peer for every remote candidate, and each call does a permission create and a channel bind, blocking, before the answer can go out. A modern browser on cellular offered sixty-six candidates from a handful of addresses — about 132 blocking round trips before anyone could answer. Permissions are now installed per unique IP.

folio  gesso  loom  /  33 commits, one new repo

A PDF renderer, in a day

On Sunday folio went from nothing to a PDF renderer, in twenty commits that read as a milestone list: the parser (objects, xref and xref streams, filters, the page tree); the content-stream interpreter drawing through gesso; text machinery and the standard fonts, with glyphs from scribe; embedded CFF and Type0/CID composite fonts; form and image XObjects; then a conformance harness graded against pdfium.

What came after the harness is the part worth noting, because it is all the material that real PDFs are actually made of: a CCITT Group-4 fax decoder for scanned pages, exact embedded Type1 charstring outlines, transparency with soft masks and blend modes, axial and radial shadings with colour functions, tiling patterns, AcroForm field recognition, a JBIG2 codec with its MQ decoder and symbol dictionaries, predefined CJK CMaps for the four Adobe character collections, Type3 fonts whose glyphs are procedures, and a fidelity tail for Type3 spacing and non-embedded CJK.

gesso grew what folio needed and no more — a per-pixel colour source across a clip, affine RGBA blits, offscreen group compositing, soft masks, and the four non-separable blend modes. And loom wired it up: application/pdf now opens inline, rendered to a scrollable canvas.

reed  /  11 commits, one new repo

Three decoders in three days

A pure Common Lisp MPEG-1/2/2.5 Layer III decoder on Tuesday, verified against minimp3 at 24 kHz once the LSF implicit preflag was applied. AAC-LC with ADTS and MP4 framing on Wednesday. Then Opus, in pieces that each stand on their own: the range decoder and packet framing, a conformance-verified CELT decoder, a SILK decoder covering narrow, medium and wideband in mono and stereo, hybrid mode with mode-switch redundancy, and Ogg demuxing to finish it. Which is to say the whole of RFC 6716.

modus  /  WS4, WS5  /  104 commits

A third architecture, and an honest verdict

Two workstreams ran in parallel. On aarch64 the runtime JIT came up in five staged pieces — translator baked in as dead code, runtime co-init, out-of-module call relocation, a constant pool, then a generic evaluator seam — and was flipped on by default for the shipping CLI, after a call-relocation untag bug that had been halving addresses. It was flipped back off two days later, because the JIT re-executes top-level forms. That reversal is recorded as its own commit rather than folded away.

The other workstream brought up i386 from nothing: seventeen missing opcodes, a hosted ELF32 target, then a CL image that boots and evaluates on 32 bits. Most of the work was width: the numeric width now follows the target rather than assuming 64 bits, the bignum half-limb width stopped being hardcoded at 62 bits, and a register invariant that had been a convention became a mechanized check — which immediately found fifteen violating opcodes and then reported an empty list.

The i386 collector was never wired at all. The GC label is only ever the struct default, no i386 arm exists in the cross-compiler, and the collect path is an invalid interrupt on hosted Linux. The bulk SIGSEGV was arena exhaustion.

modus — 0a68b3d, “proven twice over rather than argued”

Three things in that sequence are worth more than the fixes. A ChaCha20 failure was localised to bignum arithmetic above 32 bits — and then retracted: the bignum band was not broken and ChaCha20 was correct. An EDX-clobber hypothesis was pronounced dead and the round stopped rather than continuing to chase it. And when the ported collector ran but corrupted memory, it was gated off with the honest crash restored instead of shipped half-working.

Underneath, the hosted CLI got real sockets: TCP and UDP on Linux, a DNS resolver, the server side with bind/listen/accept, SO_REUSEADDR and UDP multi-peer, block storage with fsync durability, and an HTTP/1.0 client — the first time modus fetched anything from the network. Plus a long run of conformance fixes driven by alexandria, and the week’s tidiest rename: eval2 became mvm-eval everywhere.

warp  warren  /  16 commits, two new repos

A delta protocol, and a file browser

warren arrived first: a Finder-style Miller-column file browser drawn over glass and VNC, with real image previews through pigment, decoded asynchronously on a worker thread with a loading state and a stale guard, and an embedding mode so it can live in a framebuffer somebody else owns.

warp arrived the same day and spent the week deciding what it is. The answer, in its own words:

The core is a delta protocol, not a renderer: keyed, budgeted, staleness-annotated deltas over typed projections, with encodings per consumer. Present is a compression decision — which slice travels toward a consumer with finite intake — so macroblocks and tokens are peer encodings and neither is privileged.

warp — 52b2c13

That reframing — a context window is a viewport, attention is priced per token the way frames are priced per macroblock — turned most of its rules into transport rules, which they always had been. The first practical client was a live pipeline monitor, which is also where it learned what the design had got wrong; by Thursday warp implemented glass’s surface contract and could be a desktop window in its own right.

weft  glass  /  51 commits

Forms, and a scroll that costs a CopyRect

weft took the forms remainder in one wave — interactive controls with activation, focus, typing and painted state; one selection rather than two, with shift-arrows, drag ranges and tab order; and what a <select> pick means and where its popup goes. Block-in-inline hoisting per CSS 2.1 cut one page’s box error from 12,302 to 1,338. Acid3 finished the week scripted at 100/100, and the roadmap was corrected from being two phases behind reality — it still claimed “no JavaScript”.

In glass, a scrolling window on the desktop became a CopyRect rather than a repaint, a clipped rectangle fills by the row instead of by the pixel, and each surface’s poll and draw were isolated so that one bad window can no longer take the compositor down with it.
